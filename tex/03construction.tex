\section{Known Examples of Quantum Isomorphic Graphs}
In this section we give a survey on known examples of
quantum isomorphic graphs.
We start by showing that the magic square game
gives quantum isomorphic pairs of graphs and continue
with more a general construction, which does not involve 
binary constraint games.

\subsection{Quantum Isomorphic Graphs from the Mermin-Peres Magic Square Game}

It is well-known that one can construct graphs $G_\mathcal{F}$ and $G_{\mathcal{F}_0}$
based on any linear binary constraint system game $\mathcal{F}$,
such that the optimal quantum strategy directly translates to a graph isomorphism between $G_\mathcal{F}$ and $G_{\mathcal{F}_0}$,
as demonstrated in~\cite{atserias2017quantumnonsignallinggraphisomorphisms}.
In particular, we summarize this reduction for the magic square game, which falls under this class of non-local games.

First, note that each constraint coming determining the product of entries over a row or column,
say $x_1 x_2 x_3 = 1$, can be translated to constraints modulo two on the exponents,
namely $z_1 \oplus z_2 \oplus z_3 = 0$, when we write $x_i = (-1)^{z_i}$.
Considering those exponents, we construct a graph $G_\mathcal{F}$ with vertices $(\ell, f)$
coming from valid assignments $f$ for those additive constraints $C_\ell$.
For example, $(z_1 \oplus z_2 \oplus z_3 = 0, \mathbf{101}) \in V(G_\mathcal{F})$
while $(z_1 \oplus z_2 \oplus z_3 = 0, \mathbf{001}) \not \in V(G_\mathcal{F})$.
%
Next, two vertices in $G_\mathcal{F}$ are adjacent when the assignments are inconsistent, meaning that 
the assignment of the shared value is different. For example,
\[ \left((z_1 \oplus z_2 \oplus z_3 = 0, \mathbf{101}), (z_1 \oplus z_4 \oplus z_7 = 1, \mathbf{010})\right) \in E(G) \]
as the shared value $z_1$ is inconsistent.

For the other graph $G_{\mathcal{F}_0}$, we consider the homogenization $\mathcal{F}_0$ of the magic square game, which is obtained by setting 
the right-hand side of all constraints $\ell$ to zero. 
The adjacency rules for $G_{\mathcal{F}_0}$ remain the same as for $G_\mathcal{F}$.
%
Note that by construction, the graph $G_{\mathcal{F}_0}$ has an independent set of size six,
as the all-zero assignment is valid and consistent for any row and column,
which means that the corresponding six vertices are all non-adjacent to each other.
%
The statement that $G_\mathcal{F}$ and $G_{\mathcal{F}_0}$ are quantum isomorphic
but non-isomorphic follows from the two theorems below, which state 
the reductions from optimal classical strategies to classical isomorphisms
and optimal quantum strategies to quantum isomorphisms of the corresponding graphs.
\begin{theorem}~\cite{atserias2017quantumnonsignallinggraphisomorphisms}.
    Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a linear binary constraint system game with $m$ constraints.
    Then the following are equivalent:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $\mathcal{F}$ is satisfiable;
        \item The graphs $G_\mathcal{F}$ and $G_{\mathcal{F}_0}$ are isomorphic;
        \item $\alpha(G_\mathcal{F}) = m$.
    \end{itemize}
\end{theorem}
%
\begin{theorem}~\cite{atserias2017quantumnonsignallinggraphisomorphisms}.
    Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a linear binary constraint system game with $m$ constraints.
    Then the following are equivalent:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $\mathcal{F}$ is quantum satisfiable;
        \item The graphs $G_\mathcal{F}$ and $G_{\mathcal{F}_0}$ are quantum isomorphic;
        \item $\alpha_q(G_\mathcal{F}) = m$.
    \end{itemize}
\end{theorem}
When we choose $\mathcal{F}$ to be the magic square game,
we deduce that $G_\mathcal{F}$ and $G_{\mathcal{F}_0}$
are quantum isomorphic, yet non-isomorphic graphs, as there is an optimal quantum strategy but no 
optimal classical strategy for $\mathcal{F}$.


\subsection{Quantum Isomorphic Strongly Regular Graphs from the $E_8$ Root System~\cite{schmidt2022quantumisomorphicstronglyregular}}

We continue our survey with the only known example of a
strongly regular graph with a non-isomorphic, quantum isomorphic counterpart.
This section summarizes the main results that lead to its discovery in~\cite{schmidt2022quantumisomorphicstronglyregular}.
\begin{definition}
    The $E_8$ root system $\Psi_{E_8}$ consists of the $112 + 128 = 240$ vectors in $\mathbb{R}^8$, being one of the following:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $|x \rangle = \pm |e_i\rangle \pm |e_j\rangle$, for all $1 \leq i < j \leq 8$, or
        \item $|x\rangle = (x_1, \dots, x_8) \textrm{ with } x_i \in \{\pm1\} \textrm{ and } \prod_{i=1}^8 = 1$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}
From this root system, we construct the orthogonality graph $G_{E_8}$ as follows.
Let $V(G_{E_8}) := \{v_x:|x\rangle \in \Psi_{E_8}, |x\rangle \equiv -|x\rangle\}$ and $E(G_{E_8}) := \{ (v_x, v_y):\langle x, y\rangle = 0\}$.
This graph is strongly regular with parameters $(120, 63, 30, 36)$.
%
Below we recall the notion of real Pauli matrices, as these will be used 
to describe certain automorphisms of $G_{E_8}$.
\begin{definition}
    The real (qubit) Pauli matrices are defined to be the following matrices:
    \begin{align}
        I = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}, \quad
        X = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0\end{pmatrix}, \quad
        Z = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1\end{pmatrix}, \quad
        Y = XZ.
    \end{align}
\end{definition}
Recall the relations $X^\dagger = X,~Z^\dagger = Z,~Y^\dagger = -Y,~X^2 = Y^2 = Z^2 = I$.
Note also that from these relations it follows that $X$ and $Z$ anticommute, i.e. $XZ = -ZX$.
We will use these facts to show the following theorem.
\begin{theorem}
    The 3-qubit Pauli matrices $M := M_0 \otimes M_1 \otimes M_2$ with $M_i \in \{I,X,Y,Z\}$ induce automorphisms
    $\sigma_M : V(G_{E_8}) \rightarrow V(G_{E_8}),~v_x \mapsto v_{Mx}$
    which form a subgroup $L \cong \mathbb{Z}_2^6$ of $\operatorname{Aut}(G_{E_8})$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    In order to show $\sigma_M \in \operatorname{Aut}(G_{E_8})$, we observe that
    the generators $I\otimes I \otimes X, I\otimes I \otimes Z$,
    $I\otimes X \otimes I$, $I\otimes Z \otimes I$, $X\otimes I \otimes I$, $Z\otimes I \otimes I$
    are signed permutation matrices, flipping an even number of signs.
    As this preserves the defining properties of begin a root vector of $E_8$, 
    and any element $M \in L$ is a product of these generators, we deduce 
    that $M \in \operatorname{Aut}(G_{E_8})$.
    %
    Note that due to the identification $v_x = v_{-x}$ for vectors $x \in \Psi_{E_8}$,
    the anti-commuting property $ZX = \pm XZ$ descends to equality of automorphisms in $L$.
    As furthermore $X^2 = Z^2 = I$, we get $\{ \sigma_I, \sigma_X, \sigma_Z, \sigma_{XZ} \} \cong \mathbb{Z}_2^2$ for each tensor term.
    Moreover, since each tensor factor is independent of the other, we get $L \cong (\mathbb{Z}_2^2)^3 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2^6$.
\end{proof}
Next we show that two vertices $v_x, v_{y} \in V_i$ have the same stabilizers under $L$.
\begin{lemma}
    If there exists $\sigma_M \in L$ such that $v_y = \sigma_M(v_x)$, 
    then $\operatorname{stab}_L(v_x) = \operatorname{stab}_L(v_y)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    Since we have $|y\rangle = \pm M|x\rangle$ and $N|x\rangle = \pm |x\rangle~\forall N \in \operatorname{stab}_L(v_x)$,
    we see that 
    \[ N|y\rangle = \pm NM|x\rangle = \pm MN|x\rangle = \pm M|x\rangle = \pm |y\rangle, \]
    since any two elements $M, N \in L$ either commute or anticommute.
    As vertices identify the vectors up to a sign, we conclude $\sigma_N \in \operatorname{stab}_L(v_y)$.
\end{proof}
Moreover, the action of $L$ partitions $V(G_{E_8})$
into 15 orbits of size 8, where each orbit comprises of a clique, 
or equivalently of a orthonormal basis in the $E_8$ root system.
We denote these orbits as $V_i$, for $i \in \{1,\dots, 15 \}$.
Furthermore, the stabilizer subgroups of each orbit, $\operatorname{stab}_L(V_i)$,
are isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_2^3$, with the intersection
of the stabilizer subgroups of two orbits being isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_2$.

Using these facts, it is shown that there is an induced magic unitary for $G_{E_8}$, coming from 
the action of $L$. For this, we choose distinguished vertices $\omega^{(i)} \in V_i$
and define projections onto the associated vectors to each orbit. 
\begin{lemma}
    Denote $P_x := \frac{1}{||x||^2} |x\rangle \langle x|$ as the rank-one projection onto
    some root vector $|x\rangle \in \Psi_{E_8}$, and for root vectors $y, z \in \Psi_{E_8}$,
    let $M_{yz} \in L$ be the matrix under which $y \mapsto \pm z$.
    Define $u^{(i)}_{v_y v_z} = M_{yz} P_{\omega^{(i)}} M_{yz}^* = P_{M_{yz} \omega^{(i)}}$
    and form blocks $u^{(i)} = (u^{(i)}_{v_y v_z})_{v_y, v_z \in V_i}$.
    Then the block diagonal matrix 
    \begin{align}
        u = \begin{pmatrix}
        u^{(1)} & 0 & 0 & \dots & 0\\
        0 & u^{(2)} & 0 & \dots & 0\\
        0 & 0 & u^{(3)} & \dots & 0\\
        \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
        0 & 0 & 0 & \dots & u^{(15)} \\
        \end{pmatrix}
    \end{align}
    is a magic unitary.
\end{lemma}

Next, the procedure
is to choose distinguished vertices $\omega^{(i)} \in V_i$,
and show that fixing the disjoint cliques $V_i$ and
changing the adjacencies between cliques $V_i$ and $V_j$
based on the adjacencies between $\omega^{(i)}$ and $\omega^{(j)}$ results
in a quantum isomorphic, non-isomorphic graph $G^{\omega}$,
which is uniquely determined up to the isomorphism coming from
different choices of $\omega^{(i)}$.

We start by defining the graph $G^{\omega}$.
\begin{definition}\label{def:gw}
    For $\omega = \{ \omega^{(1)}, \dots, \omega^{(15)} \}$ be 
    the set of some chosen vectors, for which $v_{\omega^{(i)}} \in V_i$ for each orbit under $L$.
    Then, the graph $G^{\omega}$ is constructed by setting $V(G^{\omega}) = V(G_{E_8})$
    and switching the edges of $G_{E_8}$ as follows:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item If $\langle \omega^{(i)}, \omega^{(j)} \rangle \neq 0$,
        we copy the edges from $G_{E_8}$, i.e. we define
        $(s, t) \in E(G^\omega) \iff (s, t) \in E(G_{E_8})$
        for $s \in V_i$, $t \in V_j$.
        \item If $\langle \omega^{(i)}, \omega^{(j)} \rangle = 0$,
        we switch the adjacency rules from $G_{E_8}$, i.e. 
        $(s, t) \in E(G^\omega) \iff (s, t) \not \in E(G_{E_8})$
        for $s \in V_i$, $t \in V_j$.
    \end{itemize}
\end{definition}
Note that this procedure essentially preserves the 8-cliques
making up the orbits $V_i$ under $L$ and switches the adjacency relations 
between those cliques, such that $\omega$ becomes an independent set of size $15$, already showing that
$G^\omega \not \cong G_{E_8}$.

Moreover, even though the construction of $G^\omega$ involved making a choice on $\omega$, 
it is true that two different sets $\omega$ and $\omega'$ yield graphs
$G^\omega$, $G^{\omega'}$ that are isomorphic to each other.

Finally, the magic unitary $u$ gives a quantum isomorphism between the
graphs $G_{E_8}$ and $G^\omega$, as is shown by the following lemma.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:prod}
    Let $k, s \in V_i$ and $l, t \in V_j$ with $i \neq j$.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item If $d(k, l) = d(s, t)$, we have $u_{ks} u_{lt} = 0$ iff $\langle \omega^{(i)}, \omega^{(j)} \rangle = 0$.
        \item If $d(k, l) \neq d(s, t)$, we have $u_{ks} u_{lt} = 0$ iff $\langle \omega^{(i)}, \omega^{(j)} \rangle \neq 0$.
    \end{itemize}
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}
    The magic unitary defined above induces a quantum isomorphism between $G_{E_8}$ and $G^\omega$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    Follows, essentially by construction, from Definition~\ref{def:gw} and Lemma~\ref{lem:prod} that 
    certain products of $u$ must be zero,
    combined with the statement from Lemma~\ref{lem:prod2} that the observations from Lemma~\ref{lem:prod}
    imply a quantum isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Spherical Designs}

The root system $E_8$ is an example of a \emph{spherical design},
which are dense and highly symmetric configurations of finitely many vectors in some Hilbert space.
It is therefore reasonable to expect that other spherical designs give quantum
isomorphic strongly regular graphs similar to the example of $E_8$.
The examples that are of particular interest for this project are listed in Table~\ref{tab:spherical},
and were obtained from the search for particularily small spherical designs in~\cite{HUGHES202184}.
From it, we chose to analyse the 40 vector design in $\mathbb{C}^4$ in more detail in the next section.
