\subsection{Quantum Isomorphic Strongly Regular Graphs from the $E_8$ Root System}

We continue our survey with the only known example of a
strongly regular graph with a non-isomorphic, quantum isomorphic counterpart.
This section summarizes the main results that lead to this
discovery.
See~\cite{schmidt2022quantumisomorphicstronglyregular} for further details.

\begin{definition}
    The $E_8$ root system $\Psi_{E_8}$ consists of the $112 + 128 = 240$ vectors in $\mathbb{R}^8$, being of the form
    \begin{align}
    \pm e_i \pm e_j,~\forall 1 \leqslant i < j \leqslant 8, \quad x = (x_1, \dots, x_8) \textrm{ with } x_i \in \{\pm1\} \textrm{ and } \prod_{i=1}^8 = 1.
    \end{align}
    It is a spherical $(2, 2)$-design denoted as ST37 in~\cite{HUGHES202184}, which is the name of its automorphism group
    in the classification of finite unitary reflection groups by Shephard and Todd. %~\cite{}
\end{definition}

From this root system, we construct the orthogonality graph $G_{E_8}$ as follows.
Let $V(G_{E_8}) := \{v_x~|~x \in \Psi_{E_8}, x \equiv -x\}$ and $E(G_{E_8}) := \{ (v_x, v_y)~|~\langle x, y\rangle = 0\}$.
This graph is strongly regular with parameters $(120, 63, 30, 36)$.

% Describing the central type subgroup $L \subseteq \textrm{Aut}(G_{E_8})$ with coisotropic stabilizers
% requires the definition of the real Pauli matrices.

Below we recall the notion of real Pauli matrices, as these will be used 
to describe certain automorphisms of $G_{E_8}$.
\begin{definition}
    The real Pauli matrices are defined to be the following matrices:
    \begin{align}
        I = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}, \quad
        X = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0\end{pmatrix}, \quad
        Z = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1\end{pmatrix}, \quad
        Y = XZ.
    \end{align}
\end{definition}
Recall that the relations $X^\dagger = X,~Z^\dagger = Z,~Y^\dagger = -Y,~X^2 = Y^2 = Z^2 = I$ hold.
Note also that from these relations it follows that $X$ and $Z$ anticommute, i.e. $XZ = -ZX$.
We will use these facts to show the following theorem.

\begin{theorem}
    The matrices $M := M_0 \otimes M_1 \otimes M_2$ with $M_i \in \{I,X,Y,Z\}$ induce automorphisms
    $\sigma_M : V(G_{E_8}) \rightarrow V(G_{E_8}),~v_x \mapsto v_{Mx}$
    which form a subgroup $L \cong \mathbb{Z}_2^6$ of $\textrm{Aut}(G_{E_8})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    We first show $\sigma_M \in \textrm{Aut}(G_{E_8})$.
    
    Note that due to the identification $v_x = v_{-x}$ for vectors $x \in \Psi_{E_8}$,
    the $ZX$ and $XZ$ as tensor factors will be identified as the same when considering automorphisms of $G_{E_8}$,
    therefore each tensor term yields the group structure of $\mathbb{Z}_2^2$.
    Moreover, since each tensor factor is independent of the other, we get $L \cong (\mathbb{Z}_2^2)^3 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2^6$.
    
\end{proof}


Furthermore, the action of $L$ partitions $V(G_{E_8})$
into 15 orbits of size 8, where each orbit comprises of a clique, 
or equivalently of a orthonormal basis in the $E_8$ root system.

\section{Quantum-Isomorphic Strongly Regular Graphs from Spherical Designs}

A more recent approach to find quantum isomorphisms comes 
from the study of spherical designs.
As these spherical designs are highly symmetric,
their orthogonality graphs are strongly regular.
Furthermore, geometric conditions for quantum symmetry of
such graphs have been found, allowing to translate this
problem into one of representation theoretic nature.
We start by discussing the first and (up to date) only known pair
of strongly regular graphs, and continue to evaluate other potential candidates.

\begin{definition}
    A \emph{spherical design} is something cool.
\end{definition}


\begin{lemma}
Let $\Gamma$ be a graph with no quantum symmetries.
Then there is a bijection between 
\begin{itemize}
    \item Isomorphism classes of graphs $\Gamma'$ quantum-isomorphic to $\Gamma$
    \item Central type subgroups $(L, \psi) \subseteq \textrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ with coisotropic stabilizers,
    up to conjugation.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}
